What is tinnitus?

Tinnitus means hearing a sound in your ears or head when there is no sound outside. Many people call it ringing in the ears.

Everyone hears it differently: ringing, buzzing, humming, hissing or whooshing. It may be constant or come and go, in one ear or both.

Tinnitus is very common and is usually not a sign of a dangerous illness. But when it lasts, it can be tiring: it can disturb sleep, raise stress and make it hard to concentrate. If this sounds familiar, you are not alone.

What causes tinnitus?

Often several things come together. Common causes include:

  • Hearing that declines with age. Tinnitus most often comes with this.
  • Loud noise. A noisy workplace, concerts, or long use of loud headphones.
  • Earwax blockage or middle-ear problems (for example infection or fluid).
  • Neck and jaw tension, teeth clenching.
  • Some medicines. Do not stop a medicine on your own; talk to your doctor first.
  • Stress, anxiety and poor sleep. They may not start tinnitus, but they can make it more noticeable.

Sometimes no clear cause is found. The doctor who examines you is the right person to decide what the cause is.

When should you see a doctor?

For tinnitus, the first specialist to see is an ear, nose and throat (ENT) doctor. If needed, an audiologist tests your hearing. Do not wait in these cases:

Now

Call 112 or go to the emergency department

If tinnitus comes with a drooping face, trouble speaking or seeing, weakness in an arm or leg, or a sudden, very severe headache. If it started after a head injury. (112 is the emergency number in Turkey; elsewhere, call your local number.)

Same day

Sudden hearing loss: ENT doctor or emergency department

Hearing that drops in one ear (sometimes both) over a few hours or days. With sudden hearing loss, the first days matter. Also go the same day for sudden, severe dizziness that comes with tinnitus.

Soon

ENT doctor within a few days to 2 weeks

Tinnitus in one ear only. Tinnitus that pulses in time with your heartbeat. Repeated attacks of dizziness. Ear pain or discharge.

Routine

ENT appointment

If none of the above apply, but tinnitus has lasted for weeks and affects your sleep or daily life.

If tinnitus makes you feel hopeless, or you have thoughts of harming yourself, call 112 now. Asking for help is the right step.

Frequently asked questions

What causes tinnitus?

The most common cause is hearing that has declined with age or noise. Earwax blockage, middle-ear problems, neck and jaw tension and some medicines can also lead to tinnitus. Stress and poor sleep can make it more noticeable. Sometimes no clear cause is found. Only a doctor can make a diagnosis.

Which doctor should I see for tinnitus?

An ear, nose and throat (ENT) doctor. If needed, an audiologist tests your hearing. Based on your answers, Dingin tells you whether you need to see a doctor, which one, and how soon. This guidance is based on NICE NG155 and AAO-HNS guideline criteria.

When is tinnitus an emergency?

If you have sudden hearing loss, see an ENT doctor or go to the emergency department the same day. If tinnitus comes with a drooping face, trouble speaking, weakness in an arm or leg, or a sudden, very severe headache, or if it started after a head injury, call 112 (in Turkey) or your local emergency number. For tinnitus in one ear only, or tinnitus that pulses with your heartbeat, see an ENT doctor soon. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, call 112 now.

Does white noise help with tinnitus?

Tinnitus is easier to hear in silence. A gentle background sound such as white noise may help you notice it less and relax. It does not make tinnitus go away. Dingin's soothing sounds are tuned to the pitch of your tinnitus and are used together with short lessons in the programme.

Is there a cure for tinnitus? Is Dingin a tinnitus relief app?

When a cause can be found (for example earwax), your doctor may be able to address it. For long-lasting tinnitus, the goal is to reduce its impact on your life. Evidence-based methods such as cognitive behavioural therapy, sound support and better sleep habits are used for this. Dingin helps you practise these methods at home and prepare to discuss treatment options with your doctor. It does not promise a cure.
